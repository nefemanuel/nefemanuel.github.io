I am a DPhil candidate in Social Intervention and Policy Evaluation at the Department of Social Policy and Intervention, University of Oxford, and a member of Nuffield College. My research asks what happens to council housing, and to the people who live in it, when local authorities have less to spend.

I study how local authorities' financial decisions shape the condition of council homes in England, and how those conditions in turn affect tenants' health and their routes out of the tenure. My thesis follows households over two decades by linking the British Household Panel Survey and *Understanding Society* to local government expenditure and housing records, and focuses on the period since austerity began in 2010.

I was born and raised on a council estate in North London. My research is informed by that experience of the social, material and political conditions of everyday life in social housing, as much as by academic interest. It underpins my commitment to understanding how housing systems produce and reproduce inequality, and what poor-quality homes and disinvestment in local authority stock mean for low-income households.

Before my doctorate I worked in the UK civil service on trade policy and strategy, and I studied economics at the University of Manchester and sociology at Oxford. I co-founded and chair the Oxford Housing Research Group (OxHouse), which brings together researchers working on housing across the university. My doctorate is funded by the Economic and Social Research Council.

Recent publication
======

**Damp places, unhappy faces: local authority housing repairs and maintenance expenditure on residents' wellbeing in England.** *Housing Studies*, 2026. [Read the article](https://doi.org/10.1080/02673037.2026.2730273).

Higher repairs and maintenance spending per dwelling is associated with better mental and physical wellbeing among council tenants living in inadequate conditions. Winner of the Barnett Prize 2025 and Highly Commended for the Housing Studies Association's Valerie Karn Prize.
